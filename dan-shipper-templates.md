Dan Shipper: Content Type Taxonomy + Templates
CONTENT TYPES IDENTIFIED
There are 9 distinct content types he posts. Each has its own repeating structure and rhythm.
TYPE 1: The "NEW:" / Announcement Drop
What it is: A structured launch post for a new article, guide, or feature. Always starts with a bold signal word ("NEW:", "BREAKING:", "🚨"), followed by a short hook sentence, then a bulleted breakdown of what's inside. Ends with a linked image card.
Engagement tier: 🔥 TOP (regularly 200K–434K impressions, 1K–2K+ likes)
Engagement data from posts:

"NEW: i wrote a complete technical guide to building agent-native software" → 2.1K ❤️ / 434K 👁
"🚨 BREAKING: A new AI app by @every launches today: Monologue" → 483 ❤️ / 258K 👁
"🚨 NEW: What if your inbox was just…handled?" → 268 ❤️ / 223K 👁
"BREAKING NEWS: OpenAI just launched a new coding model GPT-5 Codex" → high engagement
Template:

NEW: [what was just created/launched]
[one sentence on who it's for or why it matters]
it covers:
- [key point 1]
- [key point 2]
- [key point 3]
- [key point 4]
[link/image card]
Variant A — Product Launch:
🚨 NEW:
[Intriguing problem question]?
That's why we built @[product]: [one-line description of what it does and why it's different]
Today, [product] is [status update]. 🚀
It does three things:
- [feature 1]
- [feature 2]
- [feature 3]
[video demo]
Variant B — BREAKING model/news:
BREAKING:
[Model/thing] just [launched/happened].
[One sentence on why this matters]
[Optional: "We've been testing it for X weeks. Here's what we found:"]
[link card or article image]

TYPE 2: The Concept Post (Big Idea + Article Excerpt)
What it is: A declarative thesis sentence that names a new concept or reframes something familiar. Then an embedded article excerpt card that expands the argument. Often co-authored or from their own publication.
Engagement tier: 🔥🔥 HIGHEST (270K–326K+ impressions, 3K likes)
Engagement data:

"we are moving from a knowledge economy to an allocation economy" → 3K ❤️ / 270K 👁
"we have completely changed our engineering philosophy because of Claude Code. We called it Compounding Engineering" → 1.8K ❤️ / 326K 👁
Template:

[bold, novel reframing of a known concept in one sentence]
[optional: one more sentence on the implication]
[embedded article excerpt card]
Variant — Named Framework:
[verb phrase that describes a philosophical shift]:
[Concept Name]:
[One-sentence definition that is pithy and paradox-like]
[brief expansion: what this means in practice]
@[collaborator] just wrote THE definitive guide to [concept]
[link]

TYPE 3: The Podcast/Interview Tease
What it is: A post promoting a new podcast episode. Opens with a hook about the guest's most remarkable quality or situation. Then bullet points on what was covered. Ends with a video embed labeled "In this episode...".
Engagement tier: 🔥 HIGH (100K–183K impressions, 270–500 likes)
Engagement data:

David Perell episode → 272 ❤️ / 107K 👁
Anne-Laure Le Cunff episode → 342 ❤️ / 72K 👁
Tyler Cowen episode → 460 ❤️ / 109K 👁
(Alex Matthew, 17-year-old AI school student) → 60 ❤️ / 17K 👁
Template:

[Full Name] (@[handle]) is [one jaw-dropping descriptor or feat].
[Second sentence that deepens the intrigue — the contradiction, the achievement, the question it raises]
[Name / @handle] [tool or approach] to [do interesting thing]:
- [specific use case 1]
- [specific use case 2]  
- [specific use case 3]
[Optional: "And we did X live in the episode."]
[video embed with "In this episode..." overlay]
Variant — The Unexpected Person:
[Name] (@[handle]) is [age] years old and [does something adults can barely do].
[Second sentence of disbelief/context]
[One more line that sets up the insight]
[video embed]

TYPE 4: The Meme / Two-Group Juxtaposition
What it is: "Group A hears news → panics" vs "Group B (tech people / people who know) → reacts with calm or excitement." Usually pairs a text setup with a meme image or GIF.
Engagement tier: 🔥 HIGH (69K–419K impressions)
Engagement data:

"investment bankers who just found out about Claude Code: SELL SELL SAAS IS DEAD / everyone in tech: [meme]" → 181 ❤️ / 10K 👁
"BREAKING: At the Super Bowl halftime show, Bad Bunny says he prefers Opus 4.6 for vibe coding but turns to 5.3 Codex for gnarly engineering problems" → 8.8K ❤️ / 419K 👁 (the highest-performing post in this category — a satirical "breaking news" culture cross)
"low key google had the best AI Super Bowl commercial for consumers" → 331 ❤️ / 69K 👁
Template A — Two Groups:

[GROUP A — outsiders/skeptics] who just found out about [new development]: [PANICKED ALL-CAPS REACTION]
[GROUP B — insiders/builders] [reaction: meme / calm / opposite]
Template B — Cultural Crossover (highest potential):
BREAKING:
At [cultural moment/event], [famous person or thing] [does something that maps onto the current tech debate].
"[made-up quote that uses tech concepts in a culturally familiar voice]" [- attribution]
Template C — The Stealth Endorsement:
[casual lowercase observation about something in pop culture or news]
[one line that reframes it through your lens — why it's actually good/smart]
[embed of the thing]

TYPE 5: The Workflow / "New Pattern" Dispatch
What it is: A personal report from the frontier. "Here's a new thing I'm doing with [tool/workflow]." Short, specific, useful. Often shares a screenshot of the actual output or interface.
Engagement tier: ⚡ MEDIUM-HIGH (2K–17K impressions, 60–200 likes)
Engagement data:

"in 2026 agent experience is just as important as user experience" → 4.3K 👁
"I've been journaling a lot with AI. It was so good at identifying my patterns that my therapist freaked out" → high engagement

new pattern:
[describe the workflow in plain language, first person, specific tool]
[what it produces or feels like]
[optional: why it works / what the payoff is]
Template B — Personal Experiment Report:
[what you did with AI/tool] 
[the surprising or delightful outcome]
[screenshot/image of result]
Template C — One-line Observation:
[declarative lowercase sentence that names a new pattern or dynamic]

TYPE 6: The Milestone / Team Pride Post
What it is: A short, warm, personal celebration of a number or moment. Usually 2–3 lines max. Includes a screenshot of the metric dashboard. Lowercase, genuine, not braggy.
Engagement tier: ⚡ MEDIUM (22K–123K impressions, 200–450 likes)
Engagement data:

"we did it!!! i am so proud of this team. we are just getting started" (MRR $100K screenshot) → 123K 👁
Template:

we did it[!]
[one honest sentence of emotion]
[metric screenshot]
Variant:
[lowercase reaction word or phrase]
[one sentence of context on what changed or what this means]
[chart/screenshot]

TYPE 7: The One-Liner React / Quote Tweet
What it is: The shortest post type. Dan quotes someone else's tweet and adds a single sentence — sometimes just two words — that delivers a payoff, endorsement, or joke.
Engagement tier: ⚡ VARIABLE (low impressions but very authentic, high engagement rate relative to length)
Examples:

"openclaw is law" [quoting Brandon Gell's post]
"good time to buy" [quoting IBM stock crash news]
"Let's goooooooooo" [quoting a new product feature]
"boom roasted" [with screenshot of AI agent making a blunt comment]
"ILL SEE YOU IN COURT" [quoting product named after him]
"So sick" / "jesus..." / "We live in the future indeed"
Template:

[1–5 word reaction in all-lowercase or deliberate ALL-CAPS]
[quoted tweet embedded]

TYPE 8: The Macro Thesis / Manifesto Post
What it is: A longer-form narrative post that opens with a bold framing sentence about how the world is changing, then builds a short argument. Usually attached to an article card with a visual/image. Sometimes posted as a standalone "X Article."
Engagement tier: 🔥🔥 HIGHEST (112K–270K impressions, 374–626 likes)
Engagement data:

"we are moving from a knowledge economy to an allocation economy" → 3K ❤️ / 270K 👁
"our master plan @every" [with visual of "We live in the future"] → 452 ❤️ / 70K 👁
Fundraise letter (Reid Hoffman / Starting Line) → 626 ❤️ / 166K 👁
Template:

[bold reframing sentence — the macro claim]
[2–3 sentences that define the old world vs. the new world dynamic]
[the skill, mindset, or behavior that bridges them]
[link or article card with visual]
Variant — The Vision Letter:
[Company/team name]:
[What changed]
[What you're now doing about it / what you're building toward]
[List of what that looks like in practice]
[Warm personal close — "Thanks for being along for the ride."]
[Branded visual card]

TYPE 9: The PSA / Recommendation
What it is: A flat endorsement of a tool, approach, or piece of content. Short and decisive. Sometimes just one sentence. "PSA:" opener is a recurring signal.
Engagement tier: ⚡ MEDIUM
Examples:

"PSA if you're iterating on front-end designs, you should try [tool] desktop"
"this is a masterpiece of corporate storytelling that should be studied for the next 1,000 years"
"Your next big business idea is hiding on the internet."
Template:

PSA if you're [doing X], you should try [thing]. it's [one word: great/wild/sick/insane].
Variant — Strong Opinion:
[declarative sentence about what [thing] is / does]
[short backing sentence]
[link or embed]


TOP PERFORMING CONTENT — What Actually Works
Based on cross-referencing impressions, likes, and retweet counts from the Highlights tab and the main Posts feed, here's what the data shows:
🥇 Rank 1: Cultural Crossover / Satirical "Breaking News" Meme
Best example: The Bad Bunny Super Bowl / Opus 4.6 vs Codex post → 8.8K likes / 419K impressions
Why it works: It hijacks a massive cultural moment (Super Bowl halftime show) and maps it onto the niche tech debate of the moment. The absurdity is the punchline. It's shareable outside the core audience because it works as pure humor first, tech commentary second. This is the rarest post type but the most explosive.
🥈 Rank 2: The Concept / Macro Thesis Post (with article card)
Best examples:

"knowledge economy → allocation economy" → 3K likes / 270K impressions
"Compounding Engineering" philosophy → 1.8K likes / 326K impressions
Why it works: Gives people a new lens. Naming something that was previously unnamed is shareable because people immediately want to use the new vocabulary. The article card gives it authority and depth. It performs across both the tech and broader intellectual audience.

🥉 Rank 3: The NEW: Technical Guide / Definitive Resource Launch
Best example: Agent-Native Architecture guide → 2.1K likes / 434K impressions
Why it works: High utility + strong signal ("co-authored with Claude" is itself a story). The bulleted list format lets people scan value fast. The branded image card adds legitimacy. This is the most reliable repeatable format for high-impression posts.
Rank 4: The Product Launch with Video Demo
Best examples:

Monologue launch → 483 likes / 258K impressions
Cora public launch → 268 likes / 223K impressions
Why it works: The 🚨 emoji signals news. The video demo collapses the explanation into something you can see. The "out of private beta" framing creates FOMO.

Rank 5: The Podcast Tease with Strong Guest Hook
Best examples:

Philosophy professor / builder episode → 457 likes / 183K impressions
David Perell episode → 272 likes / 107K impressions
Why it works: The hook is always the person's most remarkable quality, not the topic. Leading with "she's simultaneously running a business, writing a book, and getting a PhD in neuroscience" is more compelling than "we talked about productivity." The video embed gives a visual anchor.

Rank 6: The Fundraise / Company Manifesto Letter
Best example: Every funding announcement → 626 likes / 166K impressions
Why it works: People root for builders who share the real story. The combination of "we almost didn't do this" vulnerability + "here's exactly what we're building" specificity is a trust-building formula.
Pattern Summary: What All Top Posts Share

A reframable hook — the first line creates a new category or names something previously unnamed
Structural clarity — even complex ideas are broken into 3–5 points with dashes
Media attachment — every high-performer has either a video, an article card, a screenshot, or a meme image
Cultural timing — the best posts ride a moment (Super Bowl, model launch, milestone) while everyone is paying attention
Lowercase sincerity — the stylistic choice signals "i'm not performing, i'm thinking out loud"
Specificity over generality — numbers, names, product names, "we called it Compounding Engineering" beats "we changed how we work"
