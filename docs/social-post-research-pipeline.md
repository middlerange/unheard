# Social Post Research and Production Pipeline

Use this pipeline for every post taken from the Unheard queue. Work on one post per Codex thread. Review recently completed posts before finalizing a new one so hooks, sources, examples, and endings do not repeat.

## Required sequence

1. Read the source draft in Unheard without editing it.
2. Identify its central claim, intended account, audience, and any first-person assertions.
3. Research every unstable, numerical, competitive, or causal claim. Prefer primary sources: company announcements, product documentation, filings, academic or industry reports, and direct statements from named experts. Use independent evidence where a company source only proves that a feature exists or a funding event occurred.
4. Separate evidence from interpretation. Frame unsupported market-wide claims as an observation from our customers, product work, or research. Do not invent customer stories, personal experiences, metrics, adoption, or product availability.
5. Audit the draft for factual errors, stale claims, grammar, flow, duplicated ideas, and gaps between the hook and conclusion.
6. Rewrite around one defensible insight. Preserve the original thesis when it survives the audit; change or remove it when evidence does not support it.
7. Run the no-slop edit. Keep only sentences that add evidence, reasoning, lived context, or necessary connective tissue.
8. Design one visual concept that adds information rather than restating the title. Reuse the Xiaohei editorial style when appropriate. Prefer a diagram, annotated workflow, comparison, or restrained infographic when the post contains a process or contrast.
9. Produce a still image by default. Add motion only when sequence, state change, flow, or transformation is part of the argument. Motion should be subtle, silent, and useful. For social delivery, prefer MP4; keep GIF as a preview or fallback.
10. Pair the final copy and approved asset in Unheard. Verify account, platform copy, media, date, time, timezone, and approval state before scheduling.

## Founder voice

Write as a founder reporting something noticed through building Userorbit, speaking with customers, or doing research. The post should contain a point of view and the reasoning that produced it. It can be contrarian, but it must remain defensible. Use first person only for experiences supported by the source draft or supplied context. The product can appear naturally; avoid turning the ending into a pitch.

## Company voice

Write as Userorbit. Use a restrained, professional tone that teaches before it promotes. Avoid personal claims, exaggerated category leadership, invented customer evidence, and unreleased capability claims. Product mentions should be specific and verifiable.

## Structure

- Open with the strongest observation, tension, or verified data point.
- Establish what changed and what has not changed.
- Use one or two concrete examples or sources when they improve trust.
- Develop the insight through a short narrative rather than a generic framework.
- Close with the implication or a useful measure. Do not bolt on a discussion prompt unless the post genuinely benefits from one.

Do not force lists, symmetry, a fixed number of sections, or a dramatic one-line ending.

## Prose rules

Also follow `data/writing-prompts/no-slop.md` and the relevant brand-voice file.

Never use these phrases: "Here's the thing", "The uncomfortable truth is", "It turns out", "The real X is", "Let me be clear", "The truth is", "I'll say it again", "Full stop", "Hint", "Plot twist", "You already know this", "I'm going to be honest", "Can we talk about", "But that's another post", "X is a feature, not a bug", "Let that sink in", "This matters because", "Dressed up as", "Navigate" for challenges, "Unpack" before analysis, "Lean into", "Landscape" for context, "At its core", "In today's X", "It's worth noting", "Interestingly", "Importantly", "Crucially", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "In a world where", "The reality is", "Make no mistake", "Here's why that matters", "Game-changer", "Double down", "Deep dive", "Take a step back", "Moving forward", "On the same page", or "Circle back".

Avoid these sentence patterns:

- "Not because X. Because Y."
- "X isn't the problem. Y is."
- "The answer isn't X. It's Y."
- "It feels like X. It's actually Y."
- "What if X?" followed by an immediate answer.
- "And that's okay."
- "Noun. That's it. That's the thing."
- "This unlocks something. Word."
- "By the time X, I was Y."
- "The question isn't X. It's Y."
- "X. And Y. And Z."
- "Here's what I mean" or "Think about it".

Avoid tripling, rhetorical question-and-answer pairs, paragraphs beginning with "So", repeated sentences beginning with "And" or "But", em-dash reveals, explained metaphors, manufactured quotable lines, and repetitive kicker paragraphs. Never describe a process as "straightforward".

## Final checks

- Every factual claim is sourced or clearly presented as our observation.
- Sources directly support the nearby claim and use current URLs.
- The hook is accurate after the body is written.
- Founder and company versions do not read like minor edits of one another.
- No customer, revenue, performance, or adoption claim was invented.
- The visual carries information and remains legible in a mobile feed.
- Motion is used only when it improves comprehension.
- The scheduled time and timezone are explicit.
