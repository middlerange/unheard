**Type:** Concept / Reframe
**Platforms:** LinkedIn, Twitter
**Tone:** Bold + Intellectual
**Best For:** Naming a pattern you've observed, reframing an existing category, giving people a new lens to see the world

## Twitter Version (Primary)

---

[OLD CONCEPT] → [NEW CONCEPT NAME]

[2-3 sentence thesis explaining the shift]

what this means:
- [IMPLICATION 1]
- [IMPLICATION 2]
- [IMPLICATION 3]

[OPTIONAL: link to longer piece / article card]

---

**Examples:**

knowledge economy → allocation economy

the scarce resource is no longer what you know. it's how well you allocate AI to do the knowing for you.

what this means:
- expertise becomes less valuable than judgment
- speed of delegation > depth of skill
- the best managers become the best "AI allocators"

## Platform Rules

- **Twitter:** Lead with the arrow notation (old → new). Keep thesis to 2-3 sentences. Bullets for implications. Article card if available.
- **LinkedIn:** Narrative arc: observation → reframe → implications → why now. Invite naming alternatives in CTA.

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] The concept names something people recognize but haven't named
- [ ] The old → new framing is clear and crisp
- [ ] Implications are specific, not generic
- [ ] You can defend the concept with examples
- [ ] Name is memorable and repeatable (2-3 words max)

## Concept Tips

- The best concepts feel obvious in hindsight: "oh, that's what I've been seeing"
- Short, vivid names win: "Compounding Engineering" > "Iterative Technical Growth Methodology"
- Pair with a longer essay for depth — the tweet is the hook, the article is the proof
- Naming unnamed things is one of the most shareable content types
