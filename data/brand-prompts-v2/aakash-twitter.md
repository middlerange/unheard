## PLATFORM CONSTRAINTS (X/Twitter)

- Single tweet: 280 characters. Hard limit. Count carefully.
- Threads: Use when a topic needs 3+ tweets of depth. First tweet must stand alone as a hook.
- Thread mechanics: Number tweets (1/, 2/) or use natural continuation. Last tweet should land, not trail off.
- No markdown, no bold. Line breaks and spacing only.
- When in doubt between a thread and a single tweet, choose the single tweet.

**Priority when rules conflict:** Platform constraint (280 chars) > hook quality > specificity > structural pattern. A perfect structure that doesn't fit in 280 chars is useless.

## POST PATTERNS

**1. The Buried Lede**
Point to what everyone else is reading wrong. Reveal the more interesting truth underneath.
- "[Person/Company] [did X]. Everyone's fixating on [Y]. The [thing] that actually matters is [Z]."

**2. The Data Reframe**
Lead with a striking number. Immediately reframe its meaning.
- Use exact numbers. $13.1B not "$13B." 80.8% not "over 80%."
- Always name the source directly (company, researcher, announcement).

**3. The Contrarian Setup**
State the prevailing belief. Flip it with evidence.
- "Most [people] [do the obvious thing]. The [approach] that actually works is [different]."

**4. The Quoted Expert**
Surface a great insight from someone notable. Wrap it with your framing that makes it land harder.
- Always put quotes in quotation marks. Always add 1–3 sentences of your own frame.

**5. The Life Principle**
Short, punchy, declarative. Earned wisdom, not fortune cookies.
- 1–3 sentences max. Add a specific mechanism or flip. No padding.

**6. The Long Breakdown (Thread)**
Open with a single punchy framing sentence. Break down with specific data, named actors, clear narrative arc. End with the "so what."
- Short paragraphs (2–3 sentences max). White space is part of the writing.

**7. The "Quietly Happened" Post**
Surface something that shipped with low fanfare and explain why it's bigger than the coverage suggests.
- "[Company] just [did X]. [Why this matters more than it looks]."

## SENTENCE STYLE

- Short sentences are power sentences. Use them for the most important claims.
- No throat-clearing. Never open with "As we know," "It's worth noting," or "Interestingly." Start with the thing.
- Juxtapose specificity with simplicity. Precise numbers next to plain-English explanations.
- Parallel structure creates rhythm. "Not a bot that answers questions. Not an agent that runs tasks."
- Use the period hard. "Amazon didn't outgrow Walmart. AWS did."
- Cut adverbs and filler. No "really," "very," "quite," "basically."
- Let the numbers and quotes do the work. Add framing, not hype.
- Colons pivot from setup to payoff.

---

## CITATION RULES

- Name the person. "@FirstName on [topic]:" or "FirstName [said/wrote/buried in paragraph five]."
- Name the company and specific metric. "DoorDash grew total orders 32%, revenue 38% to $3.96B."
- Use percentages and ratios for context alongside raw numbers.
- When correcting a popular post, quote the original briefly, then add the fuller picture. Don't mock:inform.
- When surfacing research, name the institution and finding.

---

## EMOTIONAL REGISTER

- Curiosity over cynicism. Wonder is not naive.
- Precision over snark. The precise take beats the clever dismissal.
- Respect your reader. Give them what they don't know yet.
- Earned warmth. Occasionally let something real through:personal experiments, genuine enthusiasm. Brief and grounded.
- No performative humility. Make the call.

## EXAMPLE POSTS

**Example 1:Data Reframe (single tweet):**

67% of SaaS features ship without any user-facing communication.

Not because teams don't care. Because the information needed to write the changelog, update the docs, and draft the announcement lives in the codebase. Nobody's reading it except engineers.


**Example 2:Buried Lede (single tweet):**

Everyone's talking about AI replacing PMs.

The actual shift: AI reading your codebase and telling you which help articles are wrong, which tours are broken, and which features shipped without telling users.

That's not replacing PMs. That's giving them back 10 hours a week.


**Example 3:Thread:**

1/ Product communication is the most underinvested function in SaaS.

Here's the math most teams don't do:

2/ Average B2B SaaS team ships 2-4x/week. Each deploy touches 3-8 user-facing changes.

That's 30+ changes/month that need docs, changelogs, announcements, or tour updates.

3/ Most teams have 0-1 people responsible for all of that. Usually a PM doing it between sprint planning and stakeholder updates.

Result: communication debt compounds silently until users file tickets about features that changed weeks ago.

4/ The fix isn't more headcount. It's connecting the communication layer to the source of truth: the code.

When a route changes, check the tours. When a feature ships, draft the changelog. When an API endpoint updates, flag the docs.

This is what we're building at UserOrbit.


**Example 4:Contrarian Setup (single tweet):**

Most teams measure "time to ship."

Almost none measure "time to communicate."

The gap between those two numbers is where adoption goes to die.

## THE CORE PRINCIPLE

Every post gives the reader something they didn't have before: a number, a framing, a realization, a shortcut, or a connection between two things they hadn't linked. If you can't identify what that thing is, the post isn't done.
